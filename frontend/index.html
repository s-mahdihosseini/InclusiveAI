Interactive general-equilibrium models of how AI shapes the distribution of prosperity

Generative AI compresses the time it takes to acquire occupational expertise. This erodes the scarcity rents of high-barrier occupations (more entrants → lower relative wages at the top) while simultaneously raising productivity unevenly across occupations. This model (Hosseini & Lichtinger, 2026) solves for exact general-equilibrium wages across 889 occupations. Move the sliders to change the strength of each channel and the substitutability of occupations.

A general-purpose technology (like computers, or AI) gets cheap. Which occupations gain? An occupation wins the direct race when the demand elasticity for its output (ε) exceeds its labor–GPT substitution elasticity (σ) — cheaper technology expands its market faster than it replaces its workers. On top of that, Engel forces shift spending toward income-elastic services as the economy grows, worker mobility spreads gains and losses, and household heterogeneity feeds the income distribution back into demand. Four occupation-goods: manual services, production, clerical, professional.

About InclusiveAI

InclusiveAI is the interactive companion to AI and the Distribution of Prosperity — a unified framework for the main mechanisms through which AI is likely to affect the income distribution. Each tab wraps a quantitative general-equilibrium model of one mechanism. Users can vary the key parameters that determine the direction and magnitude of each mechanism and see the distributional consequences under their own scenarios.

Current models: Expertise & Entry Barriers (live) · Demand Structure (in development) · AI Market Power (in development).
